Seventy Days, a Third Rebuild

Seventy days ago I published the last note on this site. Today I am publishing the third rebuild. I hope you like it.

I did not plan the number. The first version existed to prove that I could put something on the internet. The second version was about making it feel like mine. This third one started with a smaller question: what should a personal site do when it is no longer a demo?

For me, the answer was to remove things. Fewer sections that perform. Fewer placeholder essays. A clearer path from who I am to what I am thinking about. The site should feel less like a portfolio and more like a desk someone actually uses.

What changed

The surface is quieter. The reading column is still narrow, the particles are still blue, and the little motto still sits under the line about being a Grade 9 student. But almost everything underneath is new.

The site is now fully bilingual, with English and Simplified Chinese pages that share one visual system. Light and dark mode follow the system by default and remember an explicit choice. The blog index is no longer a hand-maintained list; it reads from content/posts.json. A GitHub Action refreshes the contribution heatmap every morning. All internal paths are relative, so the same files work at a domain root, in a project subpath, or from a folder on my laptop.

None of that is the point by itself. The point is that I can now add a note without rebuilding the whole site in my head. The interface got out of the way.

The debt I wanted to pay

The layout still begins with Nathan Chen’s site. I have linked to it before, and I will probably link to it again. His page is quiet in a way that does not feel empty. It gave me permission to leave space around a sentence, to let a page breathe, and to trust that a personal site can be small without being unfinished.

Nathan is also one of the people who shaped how I think about LLMs. I did not copy his work; I borrowed a way of paying attention. There is a difference between copying a layout and learning from the questions a layout makes possible. I hope this version has finally moved from the first thing to the second.

A rebuild is not proof of progress. It is a place to put the next question.

Seventy days

I grew in the ordinary way: school, reading, building small things, and noticing which explanations survived contact with reality. The site is more mature because I am a little more mature. That is not a dramatic sentence, but it is an honest one.

Three ideas stayed with me. First, measure absolutely and relatively. A number without context can flatter you, and a comparison without a real measurement can mislead you. Second, a good idea is worth sharing. The scarce thing is not the idea; it is the willingness to put a half-formed one in front of someone else. Third, a peer’s advice often matters more than a lot of theory. I have learned more from people who looked at the thing I actually built than from most abstract arguments about how it should have been built.

What comes next

The old sample essays are gone. The blog is empty on purpose, except for this note. I wanted the first real entry to be about the rebuild itself, so the next one can be about something else.

I want to write about MoE routing, agents at the edge, small systems, language learning, and the photographs I bring back from the places I visit. I want the notes to be shorter than my ambitions and more specific than my opinions. Most of all, I want to keep the habit of publishing before I feel ready.

If you find a bug, tell me. If you have a better question, tell me that too. Thank you for reading, and welcome to the third rebuild.
